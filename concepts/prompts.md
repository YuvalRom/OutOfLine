## Fruit bowl / turtle

Use case: illustration-story. Create a carefully designed concept sheet for OutOfLine, a colouring book for ages 4–10 about seeing beyond outlines. Landscape format, white background, two equally sized panels side by side. Left heading "A BOWL OF FRUIT". Right heading "A TURTLE". This must be ONE ambiguous drawing duplicated with the EXACT SAME BLACK CONTOURS and positions in both panels; only flat colour fills change. Left is entirely black and white uncoloured line drawing, no numbers. Right is flat coloured reveal. Design an elegant, simple visual double reading: a shallow wide ceramic fruit bowl seen slightly from above, piled with 5-6 broad overlapping rounded apples and citrus; their combined arch and the curved bowl form a turtle's broad oval shell. A pear reclining over the right rim doubles as a turtle's rounded head and short neck; pear calyx is the small eye, not an added eye. A few broad leaves beneath either side double as flippers; these should first read as fruit leaves casually beside the bowl. The bowl base must not look like a pedestal. The coloured version uses connected moss and lime greens across bowl and fruit boundaries to unite a domed shell, olive shell patches, a lighter green head and flippers, with turquoise background shapes if helpful. Keep every line from the uncoloured drawing in the coloured drawing; do not add turtle anatomy only in the reveal. Sparse thin fruit and leaf details; thicker smooth outer contours. Beautiful playful hand-drawn book illustration, ample white space, no shading, no gradients, no hatching, no tiny fiddly shapes, no extra decorative objects. Crucially the left must read primarily as a believable FRUIT BOWL, rather than a turtle with fruit sitting on its back. This is a shared-contour optical transformation concept, not two unrelated pictures. No arrows, no extra text, no numbers.

## Mountain landscape / sleeping cat

Use case: illustration-story. Create a carefully composed concept sheet for OutOfLine, a colouring book for ages 4–10 about creative visual interpretation. Landscape format, white margin, two equally sized square panels side by side. Left heading "A MOUNTAIN LANDSCAPE". Right heading "A SLEEPING CAT". Both panels must repeat EXACTLY THE SAME BLACK LINE DRAWING; only flat colour fills differ. Left completely uncoloured black-and-white line art; right a warm cheerful coloured reveal. Invent a convincing shared-contour visual illusion: two neighbouring small triangular mountain peaks at the upper left of a rounded larger hill become two ears of a cat; foothills beneath those peaks subtly form its tucked-down head. One large rolling hill curves over the upper right, across its broad back and down into a curled sleeping body. A winding broad path sweeps in a graceful spiral around the lower foreground and curls inward as the cat's tail, wrapping along its belly. Sparse sloping ridgelines double as forehead markings; a small curved horizon seam becomes a closed eye WITHOUT drawing an obvious cat face in the uncoloured panel. A small rock can double as its nose. Keep mountain/valley/path composition plausible, not a literal cat with mountains drawn on top. In the colour reveal, unify mountains and hills into an orange and golden cat with a cream muzzle, russet stripes and a russet/orange curling tail, pale sky blue outside the silhouette. Keep all landscape lines, and do not add eyes, paws, whiskers or contours solely to the coloured panel. Rounded friendly forms with simple large colour areas, consistent clean black contour strokes and sparse finer detail. No numbers, gradients, shadows, hatching, texture, extra decorations or explanatory text. Prioritize a landscape first reading and a charming recognizable sleeping cat second reading. A genuinely ambiguous single drawing, not two separate scenes.
